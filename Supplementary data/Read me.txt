Yantai museum design review

The questionnaire dataset contains ratings for 15 architecture students and 15 anonymous architectural experts. Each person has 23 rating items. The student reference condition used photographs and renderings; experts drew on prior experience of the building. Both groups reviewed drawings and video clips of the original design, followed by VR of the adjusted design. Cross-media pairings use responses from the same person to the same item. The two groups are independent. No student–expert pairs are formed.

Anonymous participant ratings.csv records exact ratings and any interval or unresolved marks. The participant codes allow within-person comparisons. Expert and student rating matrix.csv provides the same participants in wide form. Student responses include 310 exact scores, 23 confirmed intervals and 12 unscorable marks. Table 4 gives exact-score means and denominators. Table 5 reports scores of 4–5 for all eleven scheme and tool items separately for 15 students and 15 experts, together with their combined counts. Bounds account for unclassified responses. Figures 9 and 10 show each group’s distribution across the five scale points. Table 6 groups paired comparisons by direction and indicates cases without two exact scores. A high Q17 score indicates greater physical discomfort. The distributions are descriptive. Concentration in the top categories limits differentiation among design criteria.

Run paper_ratings.py to reproduce the rating matrix, Tables 4–6 and student bounds. Run "Spatial measurements.py" for Table 3, the axial correlations, exterior dimensions and sightline measures. Run "Generate analytical figures.py" with NumPy and Matplotlib for Figures 1, 4 and 7–10. The ASCII CAD drawing and original graph attributes are supplied under Technical sources and in the graph CSVs. Original and adjusted visibility maps are Map 3 and Map 1; original and adjusted axial maps are zx2 and zx1.

The Unity project and Windows build are provided under VR reproduction, together with instructions and third-party notices. Individual participant scans and photographs are excluded from the public materials.

Expert rating sensitivity by item.csv gives aggregate counts at each scale point for the same 15 experts. The figures use these as a sensitivity comparison, with no averaging of records. Q19 has one series. The primary group and pooled counts remain those of Table 5.

Run Check spatial and paired relationships.py (Python 3.10 or later) for the two supplementary checks. Within-plan visibility association.csv describes the association of the two attributes at the same sampled positions in each plan. Tied values receive average ranks. Paired rating direction bounds.csv reports the minimum and maximum feasible counts for each direction when all respondents are retained. These are bounds from response coding, not confidence intervals. Marginal endpoints for different categories need not all be attainable together.

Figure 4 landmark analysis. Run "Check landmark visibility.py" before regenerating the figures. The point-map headers in sy.graph give a 600 mm grid and its CAD origin. Marker 1 transfers the author-identified approximate area using corresponding tread endpoints; markers 2 and 3 use tread centres. Landmark visibility.csv reports the median horizontal isovist maximum radial and visual clustering within 0.6, 1.2 and 2.4 m of each centre on each full graph. Figure 4 uses 1.2 m (two grid intervals). Landmark grid membership.csv lists every included point within the largest radius, with distance and both attributes. Anteroom registration check.csv repeats the original-plan calculation with the other reference tread. These are model locations, not participant trajectories.
